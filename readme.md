\# Project 5.1: Will the Customer Accept the Coupon?



\## Overview



This project looks at what makes someone accept or turn down a coupon offered on their phone while driving. The data came from a survey collected through Amazon Mechanical Turk and shared through the UCI Machine Learning Repository.



I looked at things like the weather, time of day, destination, passengers, and how often someone visits a particular type of business. The goal was to see which factors were related to coupon acceptance.



\## Main Findings



\### Overall Coupon Acceptance



About \*\*57.4%\*\* of coupons were accepted. The type of coupon made a difference. Restaurant and takeout coupons generally had higher acceptance rates than bar coupons.



\### Bar Coupons



The bar coupon results need another check before I draw firm conclusions. The initial analysis showed \*\*23.1%\*\* acceptance overall, with some groups reaching \*\*100%\*\*.



There is also a problem with how the groups were compared. “More than once a month” and “three or fewer times a month” overlap, so some drivers could fall into both groups.



The results suggest that people who already visit bars are more likely to accept bar coupons. Who they are traveling with may also matter. I would check the filters and the number of people in each group before relying on the exact percentages.



\### Coffee House Coupons



About \*\*51.4%\*\* of coffee house coupons were accepted. Acceptance was higher at \*\*7:00 AM and 2:00 PM\*\* than during late evening hours. This suggests that timing could help make these offers more useful to customers.

\## Recommendations



I would focus on sending coupons that match a customer’s habits and situation. Coffee offers may work better in the morning or afternoon, while bar offers may be more relevant to people who already visit bars.



Before making decisions about who should receive offers, I would verify the bar results and check the group sizes. A high acceptance rate from a small group may not tell us much about other customers.
